Science is not absolute.

In our age, science is something rather unquestionable, like a dogma. In serious talk spaces, with "rational" people, if you disagree with something that is put as "scientific" you are already out of the discussion, there is no validity in what you are going to say and usually that is comprehensible, since there are masses that want to posit ideas with no thinking involved (tradition for example) as a fact. The modern view of it sparks an unchanging world that is stuck with a sluggish thinking set with imaginary lines drawn in the sand that the supposed "experts" shall not cross. It also makes the solutions for our problems always technological or specialized be it on inventions or governments. Im not advocating for a reconsideration of some old value or supernatural belief, I want to show how our perception of "science" as absolute knowledge is flawed and to rethink itself and other concepts we need to break free from its parameters.

The problem of induction and the requirement of faith

To start crudely, I could talk about a "problem" that many people do not know, this would be the problem of induction presented by David Hume, that could be shortly explained with a quote:

"That the sun shines tomorrow is a judgement that is as true as the contrary judgement."

What does this mean? How can "the sun will not rise tomorrow" be as valid as "the sun will rise tomorrow" like it always does?
If we had a jar, with black balls and red balls, the black balls are stacked on the top and the red balls on the bottom, and we have to conclude which ball we will grab from the jar, if we start grabbing some balls we will only grab the black ones, with this one might conclude that the next ball will be black. But this is not correct since we cannot prove that the future will resemble the past, we can maybe only say that with the tests done so far as now, it will probably be a black one and yet it has the chance of being a red. But that is also debunkable for the fear of not being able to explain well enough and making this article even bigger, I will recommend you to watch this video from channel carneades.org on youtube, in there he will debunk the second claim in detail and with drawings.

We should also be aware that science rests on faith, not religious faith but faith in assumptions. If we take something seen as a fact, like the gravity of earth being 9.8 m/s/s, we rely on faith because we should assume that whoever did the experiment was correct, the scientists and teachers were nor lying nor mistaken, etc. If we ourselves make the experiment we should consider that you we did the experiment correctly, the scientific method works, that we can rely on our reason and senses, etc.

Those are the more practical problems with science, but I do not want to talk much about this, if you want to check out more I am going to once again recommend the carneades.org channel, check out their playlist on the philosophy of science.

Science depends on philosophical ideas like positivism, epistemology and also what is truth and knowledge. There is no death of philosophy, there is only a dogma around science. Imagine if we did not have epistemology, what would even be considered true and how would we know how to absorb this knowledge around us. Science is not an evil, science is a tool, but it is contingent on philosophy a branch of thinking that cannot be "scientifically proven" or is not "positivist". Stop the fetish with science and truth.

Scientific Revolutions

We are not in the end of times, things need to be thought over and changed, revolutions need to happen for a scientific progress of some sorts. Thomas Kuhn said that most science was like puzzle solving, we set our problems or objectives and with the given models we try to solve/achieve them, for example if our goal is to make a car we will use our classical branch of physics to make it, using classical mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, etc. But what would happen if someone tried to study an atom with classical physics? He probably would not be able to solve those problems, so to solve them we should have a revolution, a paradigm shift, the creation of quantum physics to guide us through our new set problem.

To assume that we can solve everything with our given models is to deny history, we cannot know whether we are really done with those scientific discoveries or rather those interpretations that we created from what exists.

Science depends on philosophical ideas like positivism, epistemology and also what is truth and knowledge. There is no death of philosophy, there is only a dogma imbued with this science. Imagine if we did not have epistemology, what would even be considered true and how would we know how we absorb and rationalize the information around us. Science is not an evil, science is a tool, but it is contingent on philosophy, a branch of thinking that cannot be "scientifically proven" or is not "positivist". Stop the fetish with science and truth!

Truth and Language

When we try to get to truth through language, we speak in metaphors, we never grasp "truth". When we try to create a concept of lets say an object observed in nature, we remove the uniqueness of this object to arrive at common grounds with another object that is naturally different from the other, individual features are ignored. If we try to describe a leaf, we gather a bunch of leaves and realize that all of them are different, all of them have their own peculiarities, and by trying to explain what is a leaf through language we cut out the uniqueness, the features of each leaf.
In a hypothetical scenario of a person that was never introduced to the names and concepts of trees, if they see multiple trees of the same species, they would see each tree as unique and different from the other ones, there are many variations, many shades, many types of trees for them. For someone that already knows the concept of tree species they would simply say that they are species "X".
Trees of the same species have different heights, different leaves, different branches, different cells, were germinated in different times, for us to classify a species we have to cut all of those details off of it, that is a limitation of trying to grasp truth via language.
The very opposition between individuals and species is antropomorphic, and truth is an illusion created by humans.

"What then is truth? A movable host of metaphors, metonymies, and anthropomorphisms: in short, a sum of human relations which have been poetically and rhetorically intensified, transferred, and embellished, and which, after long usage, seem to a people to be fixed, canonical, and binding. Truths are illusions which we have forgotten are illusions—they are metaphors that have become worn out and have been drained of sensuous force, coins which have lost their embossing and are now considered as metal and no longer as coins."

_Friedrich Nietzsche, On Truth and Lies in a Nonmoral Sense

The finding of truth with reason is related to science because the conditions that we set for our practices are the ones that are reproducible, here we draw a line and determine the rules and boundaries to what is supposed to be "reality". That does not mean that science does not work, but we must understand that it is not the observation of "what is", it is rather an observation of the lines we draw from "what is".

When someone hides something behind a bush and looks for it again in the same place and finds it there as well, there is not much to praise in such seeking and finding. Yet this is how matters stand regarding seeking and finding "truth" within the realm of reason.

_Friedrich Nietzsche, On Truth and Lies in a Nonmoral Sense

Scientific progress is not linear and is contingent on certain conditions (like politics) to direct its discoveries and uses

Science is political. That is a trait of it, this statement does not imply that it should not be, it just explains this inherent characteristic. During the covid-19 pandemic states, organizations, industries all tried to direct their efforts to combat the virus, that includes creating new laws, producing alcohol, researching for cures to be put in vaccines, products that kill the virus, research better cloths for masks... Did not it all come because of the demands of our reality? Was it not political? You can even see an economic pattern in the beginning of the crisis, some CEOs, billionaires and politicians said that despite the epidemic, workers would keep doing their jobs in the same way, the workers without much voice in the present system could not do much, there were protests and after the situation got worse those people suddenly changed their minds to fit their best profits, companies started getting much richer and many resources and studies were directed for all of the things said earlier.
It is not a matter of excluding politics from science because it comes from the people and it will serve the people (or at least who is in power) but to comprehend that it does not "grow" or "progress" endlessly expanding through all areas evenly is a huge step to the right direction to understand it.
